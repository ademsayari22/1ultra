# Offer ladder and economics

All prices are proposed quotes to test with buyers. They are not surveyed market rates, expected revenue or accepted contracts. Quote only after seeing a sample and estimating delivery effort. Keep two active pilots at most until actual capacity is known.

| Offer | Initial test quote | Bounded deliverable | Expansion trigger |
|---|---:|---|---|
| Spreadsheet cleanup | 150 TND | One file, up to 500 rows/eight columns, agreed format and duplicate rules; cleaned copy plus conflict/change list; target at most two delivery hours | Same buyer needs recurring updates |
| Checked PDF batch | 300 TND | One consistent layout, up to 20 pages or 200 rows, five data fields; source references, comparison of accepted entries, exception list and one scoped correction pass | Buyer accepts the pilot and confirms repeated volume |
| One workflow automation | From 1,000 TND | Fixed input/output, up to three transformations/checks, script and walkthrough; sample and acceptance contract determine final price | Demonstrated repeated work justifies automation |
| Scheduled support | 600 TND/month | Up to four scheduled hours for one named working workflow; scope and response schedule in writing | Buyer has a continuing need and Adem can maintain the workflow |

If the sample breaks the time/quantity estimate, shrink the batch or requote before accepting. The script/automation quote is a starting negotiation figure; do not promise a universal engine, integrations or uptime service at that price.

## Measure each job

Record preparation, delivery, checking, revisions and support time. Gross fee divided by delivery time alone overstates the economics. Calculate contribution before tax as the earned fee minus direct costs, including incremental tools, travel and transaction fees. Use total hours to judge whether repeating the job is worthwhile.

For directly acquired private buyers, propose 50% upfront and 50% at agreed acceptance. Existing institutional clients use their normal billing/approval process. Platform clients use the platform's payment route. A deposit is a cash receipt with a remaining obligation; earned revenue follows the work/acceptance agreement.

A planning illustration: a 300 TND job taking five total hours and 30 TND direct costs yields 270 TND before tax, or 54 TND/hour across that work. These are hypothetical inputs, not results or a rate recommendation. Real figures replace them after a job.

## Spend and capacity

Default to existing hardware and tools. Track existing account usage separately from new cash expenditure; zero new purchases does not mean computation has no cost. Avoid paid ads, hardware upgrades and subscription expansion until a buyer-funded need and explicit spending instruction exist.

Reserve time for sales and verification. Use a weekly capacity estimate once Adem supplies available hours. If committed delivery/review hours exceed that estimate, decline or reschedule additional work before agreeing to it.

Use a support job as a parallel income route when sales remain uncertain. Confirm actual first payroll date; advertised pay does not establish cash available this week.
