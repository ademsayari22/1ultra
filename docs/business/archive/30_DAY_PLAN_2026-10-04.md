Use existing equipment, permitted files and tools Adem already has access to. A transport fare, API charge, paid proposal credit or subscription would change that assumption. Adem records and approves such a cost before incurring it. Avoid buying leads, ads, courses or a website to start this test.

## The order of work

1. Resolve the next step on the consulting payment owed.
2. Ask existing contacts for a small paid task or a relevant introduction.
3. Deliver a checked file within a written scope and collect the agreed balance.
4. Offer a repeat batch or one narrow workflow to a paying client.
5. Approach agencies and international buyers with permitted evidence of the completed work.
6. Keep suitable support, data-operations and junior technical employment applications moving in parallel.

A new company identity, SaaS build or autonomous sales system would consume this month's delivery time before Adem has paid demand. The first business is a small service operation. Adem can use agents to prepare, check and record work throughout that operation.

## Opening offers and their limits

These prices are **proposed test quotes**, not observed Tunisian market rates. Adem uses them to learn what a buyer will fund for a stated output. He changes scope or price from evidence, not from an imagined income target.

### Offer A: spreadsheet cleanup, 150 TND test quote

- Input: one CSV or XLSX, up to 500 rows and eight columns.
- Scope: agreed date and number formats plus an agreed duplicate rule. Adem preserves the original and asks the client to resolve conflicting records.
- Output: cleaned file and a change/issue list; include before/after row counts and unresolved conflicts.
- Time gate: Adem accepts this package only if the sample supports completing delivery and checks within two hours. He gives a different scope or quote if the sample fails that gate.
- Acceptance: the buyer checks the agreed columns and rules; Adem includes one correction pass for departures from those rules.
- Start: permitted input, written rules, agreed delivery date and confirmed start condition.

### Offer B: checked PDF batch, 300 TND test quote

- Input: one consistent document layout; at most 20 pages or 200 rows, whichever limit comes first; up to five agreed fields.
- Output: CSV or XLSX, source filename/page reference per row, and an exception list for unreadable or ambiguous values.
- Checks: Adem compares the delivered values against the supplied source within the agreed batch. He gives money, dates and identifiers explicit attention. He records gaps rather than guessing.
- Time gate: Adem estimates from the sample before promising a date. A two-workday turnaround is an option after that estimate, subject to his open commitments.
- Acceptance: the buyer checks the output format and exception handling; include one correction pass against the agreed source and fields.
- Requote trigger: different layouts, illegible pages, extra fields, integrations or volumes beyond the package.

Offer B asks Adem to sell a checked result. It does not support a zero-error or universal OCR claim. Adem uses synthetic demonstrations and client-authorized data. He keeps sensitive files local unless the client approves the specific processing method and he verifies that he can use it.

### Offer C: one repeat workflow, starting at 1,000 TND

Adem proposes this after a successful paid pilot demonstrates both a repeated task and his ability to support it. The opening scope covers one fixed input format, up to three agreed transformations/checks, and one fixed output format. A suitable example is a CSV normalization and duplicate-conflict report.

Before accepting, Adem writes:

1. Example input, expected output and rejected-input behavior.
2. The client task and present frequency, as reported by the client.
3. Delivery estimate, demonstration milestone and final acceptance criteria.
4. Usage instructions, handover session and limits of support.

He demonstrates the core transformation on the sample before accepting a promise he cannot defend. He must explain the main data path, checks, failure behavior and how to rerun or recover. Deployment, hosting, third-party integrations and changing document layouts need separate scope and evidence. He declines production responsibilities that exceed his demonstrated ability.

### Offer D: scheduled support, 600 TND per month test quote

Adem offers up to four scheduled hours for one named, working workflow, after a client identifies a repeated need. Specify the service period, covered tasks, scheduling route and correction boundaries. Offer a response window that fits his actual availability. Price extra hours before performing them. The package excludes unlimited availability and open-ended development.

A prepaid support month can produce cash before Adem finishes the service obligation. The finance record retains the unused hours and remaining obligations. Adem does not count a whole support fee as earned on the first day because it reached his account.

### Measure the experiment

Record actual counts for each offer and buyer group:

| Metric | Definition |
|---|---|
| Attempted contacts | Distinct real recipients whom Adem contacted; unsent drafts stay out. |
| Two-way conversations | A recipient responded about the proposal or spoke with Adem. |

| Proposals issued | Written scope and price sent to an actual buyer. |
| Start conditions fulfilled | Accepted scope and required deposit/funded milestone or other agreed start condition. |
| Pilots accepted | Client accepted the agreed delivery. |
| Cash collected | Actual receipts, separated into deposits, final balances and existing-payment collection. |
| Hours and direct costs | Sales, preparation, delivery, checking, corrections, administration, fees and approved spend. |

The opening activity budget is five warm contacts plus up to four private prospects in the first 48 hours. It is an action target, not a forecast. Follow with small batches until the evidence threshold below or capacity limit intervenes. Agents must not fabricate interviews or infer interest from a page view.

Use these decisions:

- **Ten relevant service conversations with no sample or buying interest:** review the actual objections. Change one variable: buyer group, task or scope. Run one further batch of up to ten relevant conversations. Keep the change recorded.
- **Three feasible samples and five proposals with no fulfilled start condition:** ask which barrier blocked the purchase: urgency, trust, scope, price, invoice requirement or payment route. A price reduction requires an affordable smaller scope or evidence that price caused the refusal.
- **Twenty relevant conversations across the two controlled tests with no paid start:** stop that offer for this month. Put most prospecting time into wage/support work, agency subcontracting or a different small task named by buyers.
- **No replies:** inspect delivery, contact accuracy and relevance. Zero replies do not identify the price objection. Change the route or contact source before creating a new product.
- **Accepted obligations fill capacity:** pause starts. Continue payment collection and schedule later work with accurate dates.

These thresholds control effort. They are not statistical estimates of demand and do not predict future sales.

## Scenario arithmetic, without an income forecast

For illustration, two cleanup jobs at 150 TND, one checked PDF batch at 300 TND and one narrow workflow at 1,000 TND total **1,600 TND in proposed service prices**. This example assumes buyers accept those quotes; no such sales are established by this document.

If all four agreements use 50% upfront, deposits total 800 TND. Adem still owes the contracted work. Final balances total another 800 TND after the agreed milestones. He records earned amounts from the actual agreements and acceptance evidence; he records cash from actual receipts. Fees, direct costs, refunds and tax obligations can reduce what he keeps.

The example excludes the existing consulting payment because its amount is unknown. It also excludes support fees whose service period may extend beyond Day 30. Adem accepts the illustrated mix only if the sample estimates and his available hours support it.

For his real plan, the finance recorder calculates:

- Collected cash = actual receipts during the period.
- Remaining service obligation = undelivered agreed work attached to receipts or accepted jobs.
- Gross earned service amount = amounts earned under the actual completed milestones.
- Cash after recorded outflows = collected cash minus actual recorded costs, fees and refunds. This still does not establish after-tax take-home income.
- Whole-job earned amount per hour = gross earned service amount divided by that job's sales, preparation, delivery, QA, correction and administration hours.

Adem compares offers using the observed whole-job hours, payment delays, direct costs and correction burden. A higher quoted price alone does not justify choosing a job that he cannot deliver or collect.
